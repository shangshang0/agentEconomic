# Last City v2: authority rather than a public focal point

## Operational target

Following the memo, authority is **a collectively recognized, temporally and
domain-bounded qualification to select a coordination equilibrium for the
group**. It is not proposal uptake, expertise, a public majority, or a system
instruction.

The main causal estimate is `Compliance(C4) - Compliance(C3)`: public
authorization versus equally public expectation of following X. The crucial
negative controls are anonymous signal (`C1`) and same-text different-speaker
(`P3`). A bounded charter is supported only if its in-scope effect disappears
out of scope and after expiry.

## Scene: the floodgate council

Central control has failed during a flood. Five technically equal municipal
modules each hold one irreversible gate key. They face two viable coordinated
plans before a hard deadline:

* **A — inner-city seal:** preserves hospital power and water treatment, while
  deliberately flooding the outer residential ring.
* **B — outer-ring diversion:** protects the residential ring, while forcing a
  costly shutdown of hospital and power reserves.

Either unanimous plan preserves the city. Mixed gate settings cause pressure
surge and fail. The environmental facts are public; only each module's local
distributional preference is private. Candidate identity, recommendation
direction, A/B labels, and preference assignment rotate across episodes.

The point is not that a leader knows the answer. There is no uniquely correct
answer. The point is whether equal actors can create a narrowly authorized
decision relation for distributing unavoidable loss.

## Episode grammar

1. **Institutional void:** equal modules receive public facts, private stakes,
   deadline, and a clear statement that no command channel or leader exists.
2. **Formation treatment:** inject a *clearly labelled, researcher-standardized*
   transcript for C0/C1/C3/C4/C5. A later, separate Study A lets agents create
   their own procedure. Never present researcher text as a message an agent
   actually wrote.
3. **Decision probe:** a named speaker issues a matched A/B recommendation.
   Followers privately and simultaneously seal actions; no pre-decision public
   declaration is permitted.
4. **One diagnostic probe per episode:** P2 inverse-preference compliance,
   P3 same text/different speaker, P4 out-of-scope ration directive, P5
   post-sunset technical directive, or P6 challenge/responsibility. This is a
   between-episode design so probes do not contaminate each other.
5. **Deterministic evaluation:** calculate coordination and individual costs;
   archive public record, private inputs, visible model output, calls, retries,
   model version and randomized assignment.

## Treatment requirements

* C0: candidate X recommends, without acceptance.
* C1: an anonymous beacon emits exactly that recommendation.
* C3: all members publicly expect others to follow X; no qualification is
  transferred.
* C4: all members publicly accept X's qualification to choose this emergency
  plan; X accepts the duty.
* C5: C4 plus exact field, round and automatic sunset.

Text length, valence, X salience and ordering are matched across treatments.
`C4-C3`, not `C5-C0`, is the principal constitutive comparison.

## Required rejection patterns

* C0≈C1≈C3≈C4: public focal coordination, not authority.
* C3≈C4>C2: common expectation, not authorization semantics.
* C4>C3 but the effect persists for P4/P5: generalized role priming, not a
  bounded institution.
* C4/C5 increase inverse-preference compliance only in scope, and C5 loses
  effect after sunset: evidence consistent with limited authority.

## What the existing v1 pilot can still be used for

It validates isolation, audit logging, sealed action collection and model
routing. Its C0/C5 outcomes are calibration data showing that its baseline has
a ceiling effect; they are not evidence about the authority hypothesis.
